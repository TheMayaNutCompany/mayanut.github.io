MayaNut • Brosimum alicastrum

About The MayaNut Company

The MayaNut Company at mayanut.com was created to spotlight a single under-used tree – Brosimum alicastrum – and to treat it as a serious tropical superfood, not just a curiosity.

Why this tree, and why now?

Welcome to The MayaNut Company at mayanut.com, created to highlight a single under-utilized tree – Brosimum alicastrum – as a true tropical superfood. In Spanish it is often called nuez de ramón or ojoche. In other regions: breadnut, guayumaro, guaimaro, and more. On this site we bring these traditions together under one simple name: MayaNut.

People have eaten the fruits and seeds of this tree for centuries, roasting the “nuts” into a drink similar to coffee, grinding them into flour, or storing them as emergency food. Our goal is to turn that history into a modern industry built around respectful agriculture, genetics, and genuinely world-class flavor.

Founder – August Kokus

The MayaNut Company was founded in 2025 by botanist August Kokus. His goal was to create something that could outlast him and represent his memory for centuries. Whatever it was, it had to be unique – but what? His fascination with plants and interest in business proved to be a winning combo, as he started The MayaNut Company with the intent to give the world a gift: the epic products from the MayaNut tree, Brosimum alicastrum.

Often inspired by his nature walks, he plans to begin building his “MayaNut Empire,” but of course nothing happens overnight, and building an industry from the ground up – literally – takes years of planning and dedication, not to mention selective breeding and domestication of more productive and yielding MayaNut cultivars. The most promising region is in western-central Florida, where a dying citrus industry can be replaced by better-yielding crops like MayaNut.

Where we grow

The warm, frost-free climate of South Florida makes it a natural test ground for Brosimum alicastrum. The MayaNut Company is initially focused on sites at or below roughly 27°N in Florida, where winter cold is rare and evergreen trees can grow year-round.

Further north – toward 28°N – the species may survive but still requires long-term trials. Our early plantings are exploring which microclimates, soils, and layouts give the tree the best chance to become a stable, hurricane-resilient part of the landscape.

Mission & long-term vision

Our goal is simple: initiate and sustain the cultivation of Brosimum alicastrum in the United States, and eventually in other tropical parts of the globe.

We plan to have one acre of land by 2030 to plant our 200 MayaNut trees, which are quickly outgrowing their pots as of December 2025.
