MayaNut • Brosimum alicastrum

About The MayaNut Company

Welcome to The MayaNut Company at mayanut.com, created to spotlight one under-used tropical canopy tree — Brosimum alicastrum — as a true superfood. In Spanish it’s often called nuez de ramón or ojoche. In other regions you’ll hear breadnut, guayumaro, guaimaro, and more. On this site we unify these traditions under one simple name: MayaNut.

Founder

The MayaNut Company was founded in 2025 by botanist August Kokus. His goal was to create something that could outlast him and represent his memory for centuries. Whatever it was it had to be unique, but what? His fascination with plants and interest in business proved to be a winning combo, as he started The MayaNut Company with the intent to give the world a gift: the epic products from the MayaNut tree, Brosimum alicastrum.

History & purpose

People have consumed the fruits and seeds of this tree for centuries, roasting the “nuts” into a warm drink similar to coffee, grinding them into flour, or storing them as emergency food. Our goal is to transform that history into a modern industry built around respectful agriculture, genetics, and world-class flavor.

Building the industry

Often inspired by his nature walks, he plans to begin building his “MayaNut Empire,” but nothing happens overnight. Building an industry from the ground up — literally — takes years of planning and dedication, not to mention selective breeding and domestication of more productive and higher-yielding MayaNut cultivars. The most promising region is in western-central Florida, where a dying citrus industry can be replaced by better-yielding crops like MayaNut.

Where we grow

The warm, frost-free climate of South Florida makes it a natural test ground for Brosimum alicastrum. The MayaNut Company focuses first on sites at or below roughly 27°N in Florida, where winter cold is rare and evergreen trees can grow year-round.

Further north — toward 28°N — the species may survive but still requires long-term trials. Our early plantings explore which microclimates, soils, and layouts give the tree the best chance to become a stable, hurricane-resilient part of the landscape.

Mission

Our goal is simple: initiate and sustain the cultivation of Brosimum alicastrum in the United States, and eventually in other tropical parts of the globe.

We plan to have 1 acre of land by 2030 to plant our 200 MayaNut trees, which are quickly outgrowing their pots as of December 2025.
