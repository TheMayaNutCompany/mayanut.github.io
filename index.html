ABOUT • THE TREE, THE FOUNDER, THE LONG VIEW

About The MayaNut Company, Inc.

MayaNut comes from Brosimum alicastrum, a long-lived evergreen canopy tree native to Mesoamerica and parts of the Caribbean. For centuries, communities roasted and stored the seeds as a durable food, quietly relying on the tree when other crops failed. Today, The MayaNut Company, Inc. is treating this species as a deliberate, long-term infrastructure crop — not a short-term fad.

The company was founded by August Kokus in South Florida, with a simple question: what happens when you take one overlooked tree and give it the kind of attention normally reserved for major crops? More than 200 seed-grown Brosimum alicastrum trees are already in the ground, forming a living library of different seedlines, growth habits, and long-term potential.

South Florida is the first testing ground: frost-free, hurricane-exposed, humid, and biologically dense. It’s a good place to learn how Brosimum alicastrum behaves outside its core native range, and what it will take to design plantations that are both economically useful and ecologically honest. The goal is not just a product, but a stable, planted presence on the landscape.

In the long view, MayaNut powder becomes the simplest expression of the tree: roasted, milled, and usable in drinks, porridges, baking, and nutrition blends. Behind that powder sits a living system — plantations, genetics, and decades of trial work — so that when people drink or cook with MayaNut, they are also participating in a deliberately built tree-crop future.

RESEARCH • ECOLOGY • GENETICS • RANGE

Scientific & Research Notes

Brosimum alicastrum sits at the intersection of nutrition, ecology, and long-term agroforestry potential. This section will grow as field data, literature reviews, and Florida trial observations expand.

Names & Cultural History

The tree behind MayaNut has collected names: nuez de ramón, ojoche, breadnut, guayumaro, and more. Communities roasted and stored the seeds as an emergency food and everyday ingredient, often in quiet contrast to better-known crops.

Range & Climate Window

Native to warm, humid zones of Mesoamerica and parts of the Caribbean, Brosimum alicastrum prefers frost-free climates with deep soils and reliable rainfall. South Florida offers a test case: hurricanes, heat, and marginal cold events that push the species toward its limits.

Nutrition & Food Potential

Roasted seed can be milled into a fine powder with flavor somewhere between cocoa, coffee substitute, and mild grain. Research will clarify protein profile, fiber, and micronutrients — but the main focus is how MayaNut powder fits into real food systems instead of staying a novelty.

Genetics, Seedlines & Domestication

Current plantings track multiple mother trees and seed sources. Over time, traits such as vigor, branching, fruiting onset, and storm response will point toward what a domesticated Brosimum alicastrum plantation tree might look like — and how to design clones, seed orchards, or mixed stands.

MAYANUT RECIPES • POWDER • DRINKS • BAKING

MayaNut Recipe Concepts

All future recipes use MayaNut powder as the base. Powder can dissolve into drinks, enrich porridges, and blend into baked goods — a flexible way to bring a tree-based ingredient into everyday food.

MayaNut Morning Drink

Whisk MayaNut powder with hot water or plant milk, a touch of sugar or panela, and a pinch of salt. Think of it as a caffeine-free cousin of hot chocolate with a deeper, tree-based story.

Cold MayaNut Shake

Blend chilled plant milk, MayaNut powder, ice, and a bit of sweetener. Optionally add cocoa for extra depth and serve over ice as a summer drink that still feels grounding.

Baking Booster

Replace a small portion of flour in cookies, quick breads, or bars with MayaNut powder to add color, aroma, and narrative without overwhelming the recipe.

Key Questions About MayaNut

What is MayaNut?

MayaNut is the roasted, milled seed of Brosimum alicastrum, a tropical canopy tree. The resulting powder can be used in drinks, porridges, and baking as a tree-based ingredient similar in spirit to cocoa or coffee substitutes.

Why focus on a single tree species?

Because one well-chosen tree can anchor landscapes, diets, and economies. The goal is to treat Brosimum alicastrum as a serious, long-lived crop, with plantations and genetics to match, instead of a passing curiosity.

Where is The MayaNut Company working now?

Early work is centered in South Florida and similar frost-free subtropical zones. Over 200 trees are already planted, forming the first wave of future seed production and observation.

When will commercial products be available?

Real volume depends on trees maturing. The expectation is that early batches of MayaNut powder become realistic in the early 2030s, once there is a stable seed supply.

Is this health advice?

No. This site does not provide medical or nutritional advice. Any future products will follow local regulations. For diet or health questions, people should consult qualified professionals.
