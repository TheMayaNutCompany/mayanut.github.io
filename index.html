ABOUT – TREE · FOUNDER · MISSION

About The MayaNut Company

A long-term project to make Brosimum alicastrum impossible to ignore – as a food, a story and a living tree.

One tree, many names

Welcome to The MayaNut Company at mayanut.com, created to bring a single underused tree – Brosimum alicastrum – into the spotlight as a true tropical superfood. In Spanish it is often called nuez de ramón or ojoche. In other regions it goes by breadnut, guayumaro, guaimaro and more. On this site we unify those traditions under one simple name: MayaNut.

People have eaten the fruits and seeds of this tree for centuries, roasting the “nuts” into a coffee-like drink, grinding them into flour, or storing them as emergency food. Our goal is to translate that history into a modern industry built around respectful agriculture, genetics and world-class flavor.

Founder – August Kokus

The MayaNut Company was founded in 2025 by August Kokus, a South Floridian botanist fascinated by banyan trees, figs, and the entire Moraceae family. While exploring this family, he found Brosimum alicastrum – a tree whose fruits resemble a small fig, but whose seed has an entirely different potential.

August wanted to build something that could live on after him: a planted, living world of trees that people actually use. MayaNut offered exactly that – a species with genuine food value, deep cultural history, and enough mystery that almost no one in the United States had explored it seriously.

By around 2030, he aims to have a functional plantation of MayaNut trees in South Florida, at least an acre in size, bearing the first real harvests that can be turned into beverages, powders, flours and more.

Where we’re growing

South Florida’s warm, frost-free climate makes it a natural testing ground for Brosimum alicastrum. The MayaNut Company focuses first on locations at or below roughly 27° N in Florida, where winter cold is rare and evergreen trees can maintain year-round growth.

Further north – up toward 28° N – the species may survive but still needs long-term trials. Our early plantings explore which microclimates, soils and layouts give the tree the best chance to become a stable, hurricane-resilient part of the landscape.

Mission & long view

Our mission is simple and ambitious at the same time: make Brosimum alicastrum impossible to ignore. That means developing an entire chain – from genetics and seedlings, to plantations and agroforestry, to MayaNut superfood powders and beverages on store shelves.

We want future generations to recognize MayaNut the way people recognize coffee or cacao today: as a flavor, a story and a living tree that shaped landscapes and communities. This site is the first chapter in that story.

RESEARCH – NAMES · RANGE · ECOLOGY

Research & long-term questions

Brosimum alicastrum sits quietly inside the Moraceae family – related to figs, mulberries and breadfruit. For centuries it has been used as food, shade and forage across parts of Mesoamerica, and yet it has almost no presence in modern “superfood” culture.

The MayaNut Company treats the tree as an open-ended research project: genetics, climate limits, flavor development and long-lived agroforestry systems that can survive storms and strange weather.

MayaNut · nuez de ramón Tree superfood Moraceae Agroforestry Florida trials

Names & history

Across languages, Brosimum alicastrum carries names like MayaNut, nuez de ramón, ojoche, breadnut, guayumaro and more. Archaeology and oral history suggest its seeds and fruits have supported people as everyday food, emergency reserves and animal feed.

Range & climate

In the wild, the species spans lowland tropical forests of Mesoamerica and parts of the Caribbean. Our Florida work focuses on warm, frost-free areas at or below about 27° N, with cautious trials a bit farther north to map the real climate edge.

Ecology & structure

As an evergreen canopy tree, MayaNut can anchor multi-layered agroforestry systems. Its deep roots and strong trunk make it a candidate for hurricane-resilient plantings, shade for understory crops and long-term carbon storage.

Flavor & nutrition

Roasted seeds create flavors somewhere between cereal, coffee and cocoa – with no natural caffeine. The long-term goal is to quantify protein, fiber, micronutrients and roasting chemistry as different seedlines, soils and climates are compared over time.

All of this is early-stage. The research section will grow into a full technical archive: growth data, germination notes, Florida field reports and links to academic papers on Brosimum alicastrum.

RECIPES – MAYANUT SUPERFOOD IDEAS

MayaNut recipes & early ideas

These are concept recipes using roasted Brosimum alicastrum seed as a base. As plantations mature and more flour and powder become available, this section will grow into a full recipe library.

MayaNut Mocha (Hot)
Drink
Roasted MayaNut powder with cocoa, a little sweetener, and milk or plant milk.
  • Whisk 1–2 tsp MayaNut powder into hot water.
  • Add 1–2 tsp cocoa and sweeten to taste.
  • Top with hot milk or plant milk and stir until smooth.
Morning MayaNut porridge
Breakfast
Warm, cereal-like breakfast using grains plus roasted MayaNut powder.
  • Cook oats or another grain as usual.
  • Stir in 1–2 tsp MayaNut powder near the end.
  • Top with fruit, seeds, or a drizzle of honey.
MayaNut flatbreads
Baked
Simple flatbreads using part MayaNut flour blended into a base flour.
  • Blend 10–20% MayaNut flour with wheat or another flour.
  • Add salt and water, knead into a soft dough.
  • Roll thin and cook on a hot pan until lightly browned.
MayaNut jelly concept
Fruit
A future recipe using the sweet pulp of the ripe fruit as a base.
  • Pulp from ripe MayaNut fruits (future harvests).
  • Cook gently with sugar and a splash of citrus.
  • Set with pectin into a spreadable jelly.
Iced MayaNut latte
Cold drink
Cold, non-caffeinated drink using roasted MayaNut and milk.
  • Shake cold milk with 1–2 tsp MayaNut powder.
  • Add vanilla, sweetener, and ice.
  • Serve in a tall glass; dust the top with a little extra powder.
Future bakery concepts
Concept
As seed supply scales, we plan cookies, loaves, and snack bars.
  • Blend MayaNut flour into cookie or loaf recipes.
  • Balance moisture and structure as we learn its baking behavior.
  • Share the best results in a dedicated recipe hub.
FAQ – QUESTIONS ABOUT THE TREE & PROJECT

MayaNut FAQ

Quick answers on the tree, the seed, the project, and where this is all going. Left side: common questions. Right side: a small FAQ assistant focused on Brosimum alicastrum.

Common questions

  • What is MayaNut? A working name for roasted seed from the tropical tree Brosimum alicastrum, treated here as a potential superfood ingredient.
  • Is MayaNut caffeine-free? Yes. Roasted MayaNut has a coffee-like aroma but contains no natural caffeine, making it suitable for evening drinks or caffeine-sensitive people.
  • Where does the tree grow naturally? It is native to parts of Mesoamerica and the Caribbean. Our work explores how it performs in Florida and other frost-free regions.
  • Is this a real company or just a concept? The MayaNut Company is a real, long-term project with hundreds of young trees already planted and a focus on genetics, field data and future products.
  • Can I buy MayaNut products yet? Not yet. The Store section shows concept products and brand language while groves and supply are still being built. Commercial sales are planned later in the decade.
  • Is this medical or health advice? No. This site is for information and research only. Nothing here is intended as medical, nutritional, or health advice.
  • How can I follow the project? Watch this site for updates and, in the future, social channels and mailing lists that will be linked from the Contact section.
