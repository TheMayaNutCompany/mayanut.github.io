ABOUT • THE TREE • THE PROJECT

About The MayaNut Company, Inc.

MayaNut comes from Brosimum alicastrum, a tropical evergreen tree native to Mesoamerica and nearby regions. Its seeds were roasted, ground, and stored by past cultures as a stable food source. The goal of The MayaNut Company, Inc. is to translate that quiet history into a modern, climate-resilient tree crop.

The company was founded by August Kokus in South Florida, where more than 200 seed-grown Brosimum alicastrum trees are being tracked as a living experiment. The long view is simple: develop MayaNut powder and tree plantings together — not as a short-term trend, but as infrastructure for the coming decades.

RESEARCH • ECOLOGY • GENETICS • RANGE

Scientific & Research Notes

Brosimum alicastrum sits at the crossing of nutrition, ecology, and long-lived tree crops. This section will expand as field data from South Florida and literature reviews accumulate.

Names & Cultural History

The tree is known as nuez de ramón, ojoche, breadnut, and more. Communities roasted and stored the seeds as a quiet, dependable food that filled gaps when other crops failed.

Range & Climate Window

Brosimum alicastrum is native to warm, frost-free regions of Mesoamerica and the Caribbean. The MayaNut Company is using South Florida as a testbed to understand wind, soil, and spacing before larger plantation designs.

Nutrition & Powder Potential

Roasted MayaNut seed can be milled into powder that behaves somewhere between cocoa, coffee substitute, and mild grain flour. Ongoing work will clarify macronutrients, fiber fractions, and how to best formulate drinks and foods.

Genetics, Seedlines & Domestication

Each seed-grown tree in South Florida is effectively a data point: tracking vigor, branching, storm response, and future fruiting patterns. Over time, that informs what a domesticated plantation MayaNut tree should look like.

RECIPES • DRINKS • POWDER IN FOOD

MayaNut Recipe Concepts

All future recipes assume a core ingredient: MayaNut powder. The exact formulas will change as we learn more, but these show how the powder can behave in drinks and food.

Hot MayaNut Drink

Whisk MayaNut powder into hot water or plant milk with a little sugar and a pinch of salt. Think of it as a caffeine-free cousin to cocoa.

Cold MayaNut Shake

Blend chilled milk or plant milk, ice, sweetener, and MayaNut powder. Optional cocoa or spices can push it towards dessert or breakfast.

Baking Booster

Replace a small fraction of flour in cookies, bars, or quick breads with MayaNut powder. It adds color, story, and tree-based depth without taking over the recipe.

Key Questions About MayaNut

What is MayaNut?

MayaNut is the roasted, milled seed of Brosimum alicastrum, a tropical canopy tree. The powder can be used in drinks, porridges, and baked goods — similar in spirit to cocoa or coffee substitutes.

Why focus on a single tree species?

Because one well-chosen tree can reshape landscapes, diets, and economies. The goal is to treat Brosimum alicastrum as long-term infrastructure, not a short-lived trend.

Where is The MayaNut Company working now?

Early trials are in South Florida and other frost-free subtropical locations. Over 200 seed-grown trees are already in the ground as a long-term living experiment.

When will products be available?

Real volumes depend on tree growth. The realistic window is the early 2030s, once plantations yield dependable seed for MayaNut powder.

Is this health or medical advice?

No. This site does not give medical or nutritional advice. Any future products will follow local regulations, and individuals should consult qualified professionals for health decisions.
