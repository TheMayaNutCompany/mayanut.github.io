ABOUT MAYANUT • THE TREE & THE VISION

About The MayaNut Company, Inc.

MayaNut comes from Brosimum alicastrum, a tropical evergreen tree native to Mesoamerica and nearby regions. Its seeds were roasted, ground, and stored by past cultures as a stable food source. The goal of The MayaNut Company, Inc. is to translate that quiet history into a modern, highly productive super-crop for all to enjoy.

The company was founded by August Kokus of South Florida. As of December 2025, we currently have more than 200 seed-grown Brosimum alicastrum saplings waiting to be planted as America's newest superfood. The long view is simple: develop MayaNut powder and tree plantings together — not as a short-term trend, but as infrastructure for the coming decades. August wants to create something original, new, and ever-lasting to leave proof of his existence. Call it love or obsession — nevertheless, The MayaNut Company, Inc. is passionately delighted to introduce you to MayaNut.

RESEARCH • ECOLOGY • GENETICS • RANGE

Scientific & Research Notes

Brosimum alicastrum sits at the intersection of nutrition, ecology, and long-term agroforestry potential. This section will expand as field data, literature reviews, and South Florida trial results grow.

Names & Cultural History

Known as nuez de ramón, ojoche, breadnut, and other regional names, Brosimum alicastrum quietly supported communities for centuries. Seeds were roasted, ground, and stored as a durable food that could bridge gaps when annual crops failed — a hidden layer in past food systems.

Range & Climate Window

Native to Mesoamerica and parts of the Caribbean, Brosimum alicastrum thrives in warm, frost-free climates. The initial focus is South Florida and similar latitudes where long growing seasons, deep soils, and humidity can support tall, hurricane-resistant trees and future seed production.

Nutrition & Food Potential

Roasted MayaNut seed can be milled into powder that behaves somewhere between cocoa, a coffee substitute, and a mild grain flour. Ongoing work will clarify protein profile, fiber fractions, micronutrients, and how best to formulate drinks, porridges, and baked products at scale.

Genetics, Seedlines & Domestication

Current plantings track seedlings from multiple mother trees to document traits such as vigor, branching, canopy shape, fruiting onset, and response to wind. Over time this will inform what a domesticated plantation tree of Brosimum alicastrum should look like and how MayaNut fits into diversified, climate-resilient farms.

MAYANUT RECIPES • POWDER • DRINKS • BAKING

MayaNut Recipe Concepts

All future recipes will use MayaNut powder as the base — not flour alone. Powder can dissolve in drinks, enrich porridges, and blend into baked goods. These ideas are starting points, not final formulas.

MayaNut Morning Drink

Whisk MayaNut powder with hot water or plant milk, a touch of sugar or panela, and a pinch of salt. Think of it as a caffeine-free cousin of hot chocolate with its own forest-floor depth.

Cold MayaNut Shake

Blend chilled plant milk, MayaNut powder, ice, a little sweetener, and cocoa if desired. Serve over ice for a summer drink that still feels grounding and tree-based.

Baking Booster

Swap a small portion of flour in cookies, bars, or quick breads with MayaNut powder to add flavor, color, and a story — without overwhelming the recipe.

Key Questions About MayaNut

What is MayaNut?

MayaNut is the roasted, milled seed of Brosimum alicastrum, a tropical canopy tree. The powder can be used in drinks, porridges, and baked goods, similar in spirit to cocoa or coffee substitutes.

Why focus on a single tree species?

Because one well-chosen tree can reshape landscapes, diets, and economies. The work here treats Brosimum alicastrum as a long-lived infrastructure crop — not a short trend.

Where is The MayaNut Company working now?

Early trials are in South Florida and other frost-free subtropical locations. Over 200 trees are already in the ground, growing toward future seed production.

When will commercial products be available?

The goal is meaningful harvests in the early 2030s. Before that, the focus is genetics, plantation design, and recipes using MayaNut powder.

Is this health advice?

No. This site does not provide medical or nutritional advice. Any future products will follow local regulations, and people should consult qualified professionals for dietary or health questions.
