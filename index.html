About the Tree · Brosimum alicastrum

About The MayaNut Company, Inc.

A Forgotten Tree with Future Potential

MayaNut comes from Brosimum alicastrum, a tall evergreen canopy tree native to Mesoamerica and parts of the Caribbean. For centuries, communities roasted and stored the seeds as a durable food source. Today, our focus is to reintroduce it as a climate-resilient, tree-based superfood.

A Long-Term Experiment in Florida

The MayaNut Company, Inc. is based in South Florida, where over 200 seed-grown trees are being established. Each planting is part of a long-term trial to understand wind resistance, branching, fruiting age, and how this species behaves as a deliberate crop — not just a wild tree.

Founder – August Kokus

The project was founded by August Kokus, a grower obsessed with banyans, figs, and the deeper potential of the Moraceae family. MayaNut is the attempt to do something rare: turn a little-known tree into a real, planted, useful part of future food systems.

Research · Ecology · Genetics · Range

Scientific & Field Notes

Brosimum alicastrum sits at the edge of regenerative agriculture and long-term tree crops. This section will grow as trials, lab data, and collaborations expand.

Names & Cultural Uses

Also known as nuez de ramón, ojoche, and breadnut, the tree has quietly supported communities with seeds that can be roasted, ground, or stored as food when annual crops fail.

Range & Climate Window

Native to warm, humid regions of Mesoamerica, MayaNut appears best suited to frost-free subtropics and tropics. Our early focus is South Florida — a test bed for wind, water, and soil conditions that a commercial plantation would have to survive.

Nutrition & Powder Behavior

Roasted seeds milled into MayaNut powder behave somewhere between cocoa, coffee substitute, and a mild cereal flour. Future work will clarify protein, fiber, and how best to formulate drinks, porridges, and baked foods.

Seedlines, Genetics & Domestication

Each planted tree is tagged by source. Over years, we will compare vigor, architecture, fruiting onset, and stress tolerance. The end goal is a small set of reliable, domesticated MayaNut types that can support real farms and real products.

MayaNut Recipes · Powder · Drinks · Baking

MayaNut Recipe Concepts

In the future, most recipes will center on MayaNut powder — a roasted, caffeine-free seed powder that can move between drinks, porridges, and baked goods. These concepts are placeholders for what real formulation work will refine.

MayaNut Morning Drink

Whisk MayaNut powder with hot water or plant milk, plus a little sweetener and a pinch of salt. Think of it as a forest-based morning drink — deep, grounding, and caffeine-free.

Cold MayaNut Shake

Blend chilled plant milk, MayaNut powder, ice, and a touch of sugar or panela. Serve over ice as a summer drink that still feels like it came from a tree, not a factory.

Baking Booster

Replace a small portion of flour in cookies, loaves, or bars with MayaNut powder to add flavor, color, and a story without overwhelming the recipe. Final ratios will come from real-world tests.

Core Questions

What is MayaNut?

MayaNut is the roasted, milled seed of Brosimum alicastrum — a tropical canopy tree. The powder can be used in drinks, porridges, and baked goods, similar in spirit to cocoa or coffee substitutes, but from a different tree and story.

Why focus on one tree species?

Because one carefully chosen tree can reshape landscapes, diets, and economies over decades. This project treats Brosimum alicastrum as a long-lived infrastructure crop — not a quick product trend.

Where is The MayaNut Company working now?

Early trials are in South Florida and other frost-free locations. More than 200 trees are already in the ground, growing toward future seed production and real-world data.

When will products be available?

Tree crops move on tree time. The ambition is meaningful harvests in the early 2030s. Before that, the focus is genetics, plantation design, and honest recipe development with MayaNut powder.

Is this health advice?

No. This site does not provide medical or nutritional advice. Any future products will follow regulations in their region, and individuals should consult qualified professionals regarding diet or health.
