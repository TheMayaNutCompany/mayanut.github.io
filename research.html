Research on MayaNut™ (Brosimum alicastrum)

Botanical Overview

MayaNut™ (Brosimum alicastrum) is a long-lived evergreen tropical food tree in the Moraceae family. Its structure, canopy habit, and perennial life strategy make it a candidate for long-horizon evaluation where durable, low-replant systems are desirable.

This research focuses on cultivation performance and practical field behavior, especially where Florida agriculture provides a new environment for comparison.

Food Characteristics: Fruit and Seed

The tree produces a fleshy fruit containing a single large seed. While the fruit can be eaten fresh, the edible tree-seed is the most practical component for storage and processing, which is why MayaNut™ is evaluated as a perennial crop ingredient rather than a short-shelf-life fruit product.

Seed traits of interest include size consistency, density, drying behavior, and suitability for roasting and milling as the project develops.

Germination and Early Growth Behavior

Brosimum alicastrum can germinate quickly under favorable conditions, and the species is known for hypogeal germination where cotyledons remain protected below the soil surface. Early vigor is monitored because establishment success heavily determines outcomes in seed-grown trees intended for long-term cultivation.

Key observations include sprouting speed, initial stem development, leaf flush timing, and how young trees respond to container culture and transplanting.

Seed-Grown Populations and Genetic Diversity

The project emphasizes seed-grown populations to preserve genetic diversity. Variation among seed-grown trees can reveal differences in growth rate, form, and stress response, which becomes actionable information for selection and domestication rather than noise to be eliminated.

This approach supports responsible evaluation of a tropical food tree across microclimates and soils common in Florida agriculture.

Growth Characteristics and Environmental Tolerance

Once established, MayaNut™ is evaluated for durability under variable moisture, heat, and wind exposure. Growth performance is tracked as a function of soil drainage, irrigation strategy during establishment, and seasonal temperature patterns.

The goal is to identify consistent performance traits for a perennial crop pathway without over-claiming outcomes before trees mature.

Geographic Distribution and Caribbean Presence

Native range is generally described across parts of southern Mexico and Central America, with established Caribbean presence documented in locations such as Jamaica and Cuba. Regional context is used to frame climate expectations while recognizing that local adaptation can differ meaningfully across populations.

Distribution research supports responsible evaluation when studying Brosimum alicastrum outside its traditional cultivation footprint.

Florida as a Research Context

Florida offers a practical test environment for a seed-grown perennial crop because small shifts in temperature, rainfall timing, and soil type can change performance. Observations focus on survivability, growth behavior, and long-term maintenance requirements under real cultivation conditions.

This page remains the long-form reference for MayaNut™ research updates as the work progresses.

Global Potential in Tropical Regions

Broader interest includes how a tropical food tree might perform in suitable tropical regions worldwide where long-lived, seed-producing canopy trees can support resilient food systems. Any global evaluation should be based on localized trials, ecological safeguards, and long-horizon data.

This section is intentionally conservative: potential is discussed as research direction, not guaranteed commercial outcome.

The MayaNut Company and Domestication Efforts

The MayaNut Company is documenting cultivation and growth traits to build a practical baseline for domestication. The emphasis remains on seed-grown trees, observation-driven selection, and stewardship consistent with the realities of long-lived perennial crop development.

For background, see About the project, and for questions, see the FAQ.

Current Research Status and Outlook

Current work remains in an early observational phase. As trees mature, research may expand into flowering and seed-set patterns, seed processing characteristics, and selection criteria for high-performing seed-grown trees suited to Florida agriculture.
