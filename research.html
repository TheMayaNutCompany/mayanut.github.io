Botanical Overview

MayaNut™ (Brosimum alicastrum) is a long-lived evergreen tropical food tree in the Moraceae family. It is structurally robust, capable of forming a dense canopy at maturity, and known for developing a strong, extensive root system that supports long-term site stability. These characteristics align with species that evolved to persist for decades rather than complete a short cycle, which is why it is being evaluated as a perennial crop candidate.

Current research emphasizes cultivation, growth behavior, and environmental responses—especially where subtropical conditions create new questions for Florida agriculture.

Food Characteristics: Fruit and Seed

The tree produces a fleshy fruit containing a single large seed. While the fruit itself is edible and is often described as syrupy-sweet with tangy fig-like notes, it is the seed—referred to here as MayaNut™—that has historically been the most valued food component. The edible tree-seed is dense, durable, and suitable for drying and processing, which makes it far more practical for storage and long-term use than the highly perishable fruit.

This seed-centered practicality is a major reason Brosimum alicastrum remains a compelling research subject for long-horizon food-tree systems.

Germination and Early Growth Behavior

A notable trait of Brosimum alicastrum is rapid and reliable germination under favorable conditions. The species produces hypogeal seeds, meaning the cotyledons remain below the soil surface during germination, offering protection to the developing seedling. Seeds may sprout in under one week, and early growth can be vigorous during the first year from seed.

Because establishment success strongly influences outcomes for any perennial crop, documenting germination speed, early vigor, and juvenile growth behavior is central to MayaNut™ research.

Seed-Grown Populations and Genetic Diversity

Research by The MayaNut Company prioritizes seed-grown trees rather than clonal or grafted material. Seed-grown populations preserve genetic diversity, which is critical when evaluating a species for adaptation to new environments. Genetic variability influences growth rate, structural form, stress tolerance, and reproductive behavior.

Rather than attempting to eliminate variation, current evaluation treats diversity as essential data for future selection and domestication efforts—especially when assessing a tropical food tree in subtropical cultivation.

Growth Characteristics and Environmental Tolerance

Once established, MayaNut™ (Brosimum alicastrum) can tolerate variable rainfall patterns and extended dry periods. Its evergreen nature allows year-round photosynthesis, supporting steady biomass accumulation. The tree’s robust structure and extensive root system align with long-lived canopy species that persist across time rather than rely on repeated replanting.

In cultivation, individual trees may show meaningful differences in form and growth rate influenced by soil structure, drainage, and establishment conditions. These variables are monitored to understand the practical range of performance traits in seed-grown populations.

Geographic Distribution and Caribbean Presence

Brosimum alicastrum is native to parts of Central America and southern Mexico, with broader presence across the Caribbean. Established populations are known in Jamaica and Cuba, where the species has adapted to local conditions. In other regions, including Haiti, the species is often described as introduced rather than naturally occurring.

Understanding geographic distribution helps frame expectations for climate tolerance and guides responsible evaluation when a long-lived food tree is studied beyond its traditional range.

Florida as a Research Context

Florida provides a relevant environment for evaluating MayaNut™ as a long-term agricultural crop beyond its traditional range. The state’s climate, combined with increasing pressure on established perennial systems, creates an opportunity to explore alternative tree-based food crops using careful observation rather than short-term assumptions.

Current work in Florida focuses on establishment success, growth behavior, and survivability. No assumptions are made regarding yield or commercial scalability at this stage; the project is centered on building a reliable baseline for a seed-grown perennial crop.

Global Potential in Tropical Regions

Beyond the Americas, Brosimum alicastrum may hold potential in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide where long-lived, resilient food trees are increasingly valued. Climatically suitable areas in Africa, Southeast Asia, India, northern Australia, and parts of Oceania may warrant exploratory evaluation, subject to region-specific research, ecological assessment, and responsible introduction practices.

Any global potential remains exploratory and depends on local conditions, biodiversity safeguards, and long-horizon trials.

The MayaNut Company and Domestication Efforts

The MayaNut Company is actively engaged in early-stage research and domestication efforts centered on MayaNut™. Rather than treating Brosimum alicastrum as a short-term crop, the project emphasizes long time horizons, careful observation, and biological stewardship—reflecting the realities of tree-based food systems where meaningful conclusions emerge over years rather than seasons.

This work includes maintaining seed-grown populations, tracking growth and environmental responses, and building foundational knowledge necessary for responsible selection and future domestication.

Current Research Status and Outlook

At present, MayaNut™ research remains in an early observational phase. Trees are being cultivated and monitored to establish baseline understanding of growth patterns, environmental tolerance, and long-term survivability.

Future research may explore reproductive consistency, seed processing characteristics, and selection strategies as trees mature. These efforts reflect the inherently long timelines required to evaluate a long-lived tropical food tree as a perennial crop.
