Research Reference — MayaNut

Practical cultivation behavior, repeatable seed traits, and Florida-based observations for long-horizon perennial development of Brosimum alicastrum.

RESEARCH FILE
Research Overview
Baseline, purpose, method

MayaNut (Brosimum alicastrum) is a long-lived evergreen tropical food tree in the Moraceae family. Research focuses on durability, low replant frequency, and stable long-horizon performance.

RESEARCH FILE
Food Characteristics
Fruit, seed, processing

The tree produces a fleshy fruit containing a single large seed. The seed is the primary research focus for storage and processing: drying behavior, roasting response, milling behavior, and product versatility.

Roasted seeds typically develop a deeper aromatic profile suitable for multiple uses.

Traits Tracked
Seed + growth metrics
  • Seed size and uniformity across trees
  • Drying behavior and storage practicality
  • Roasting response and milling behavior
  • Germination and early growth behavior
RESEARCH FILE
Early-Stage Observations
Seedlings + containers
  • Days to sprout and emergence uniformity
  • Leaf flush timing and recovery after handling
  • Root and soil-moisture sensitivity
  • Stem thickening and branching habit
RESEARCH FILE
Seed-Grown Populations
Variation + selection

Research emphasizes seed-grown populations to preserve genetic diversity. Variation in growth rate, structure, and stress response is treated as signal for selection rather than removed prematurely.

RESEARCH FILE
Growth & Tolerance
Heat, wind, moisture

Once established, MayaNut is evaluated for durability under variable moisture, heat, and wind exposure. Observations focus on survivability, growth behavior, and maintenance needs under real Florida conditions.

RESEARCH FILE
Geographic Context
Range + climate framing

Native range spans southern Mexico and Central America, with Caribbean presence in Jamaica and Cuba. Regional context helps frame climate expectations while recognizing local adaptation across populations.

RESEARCH FILE
Florida Trials
Soils + seasonality

Florida provides a practical test environment where small shifts in temperature, rainfall timing, and soil type can meaningfully affect performance. Observations prioritize survivability and long-term practicality.

RESEARCH FILE
Status & Outlook
Next milestones

Research remains in an early observational phase. As trees mature, future work may expand into flowering behavior, seed-set patterns, processing characteristics, and identification of consistently high-performing trees suited to Florida agriculture.
