Research Reference — MayaNut™

Practical cultivation behavior, repeatable seed traits, and Florida-based observations for long-horizon perennial development of Brosimum alicastrum.

Research Overview

MayaNut™ (Brosimum alicastrum) is a long-lived evergreen tropical food tree in the Moraceae family. Its perennial structure, canopy form, and extended lifespan make it relevant to crop systems where durability, low replant frequency, and stable infrastructure matter.

Food Characteristics

The tree produces a fleshy fruit containing a single large seed. While the fruit can be eaten fresh, the seed is the primary research focus for storage and processing. Traits evaluated include size distribution, density, drying behavior, and performance after roasting and milling.

Growers commonly describe the fruit as sweet and syrupy, while roasted seeds develop a more aromatic, versatile profile.

Traits Tracked Over Time

  • Seed size and uniformity across trees
  • Drying behavior and storage practicality
  • Roasting response and milling behavior
  • Germination and early growth behavior

Early-Stage Observations

  • Days to sprout and emergence uniformity
  • Leaf flush timing and recovery after handling
  • Root and soil-moisture sensitivity
  • Stem thickening and branching habit

Seed-Grown Populations

Research emphasizes seed-grown populations to preserve genetic diversity. Variation in growth rate, structure, and stress response is treated as useful signal for selection and domestication rather than removed prematurely.

Growth & Tolerance

Once established, MayaNut™ is evaluated for durability under variable moisture, heat, and wind exposure. Observations focus on survivability, growth behavior, and maintenance needs under real Florida conditions.

Geographic Context

The native range spans southern Mexico and Central America, with documented Caribbean presence in Jamaica and Cuba. Regional context helps frame climate expectations while recognizing that local adaptation varies across populations.

Florida as a Test Environment

Florida provides a practical test environment where small shifts in temperature, rainfall timing, and soil type meaningfully affect performance. Observations prioritize survivability and long-term practicality.

Global Direction

Broader interest includes evaluating how long-lived tropical food trees may support resilient food systems globally. Any expansion must rely on localized trials, ecological safeguards, and long-horizon data.

Research remains in an early observational phase. As trees mature, future work may expand into flowering behavior, seed-set patterns, processing characteristics, and identification of consistently high-performing trees suited to Florida agriculture.
